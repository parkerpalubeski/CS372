# Why can’t we just call recv() on all the connected sockets? What does select() buy us?
We can't call recv() on all the connected sockets because it waits until data arrives. It doesn't back out when it doesn't. So if you call recv() on a connected socket, all other sockets will not have their data received until that connected socket has sent data. If they don't, it waits forever.

# When select() shows a socket “ready-to-read”, what does it mean if the socket is a listening socket versus a non-listening socket?
In a listening socket, it means an incoming connection is sitting in the accept() queue. 
In a non-listening socket, it means data is ready to be recv()'d. 

# Why do we have to add the listener socket to the set, anyway? Why not just call accept() and then call select()?
If accept() is called before select, accept() will wait until a new incoming client connects, freezing the entire server program.
